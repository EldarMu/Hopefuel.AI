I'm 41 and I never told anyone.

I grew up falling through the cracks, slipping from grade to grade because nobody wanted to deal with the kid who couldn't keep up. By high school I learned how to bluff, memorized logos, had friends order for me, and faked bad eyesight when someone handed me paperwork. The hardest part wasn't being unable to read—it was the shame. You can't walk into an adult basic literacy center without sitting in a room where every single person knows you can't read a children's book.

Two years ago I got a cheap phone and started using voice mode on an AI app. Nobody was watching. Nobody sighed when I asked what the same vowel sound meant for the hundredth time. It didn't get tired, it didn't look at me with pity, and it never mentioned it to anyone. We started with phonics, sounding out road signs, letters on cereal boxes, then short paragraphs.

Last night I took my eight-year-old daughter out for dinner. For the first time in her life, I didn't say "what looks good to you?" so she'd read it out. I picked up the laminated menu, read through the specials out loud, and ordered without guessing. Nobody knows I couldn't read except the phone in my pocket.
