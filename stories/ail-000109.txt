My mother passed away from ALS four months ago. The only recordings I had of her speaking before she lost her voice were twelve audio memos saved on an old Android phone from 2017 with a cracked digitizer and a corrupt internal flash partition that entered a bootloop.

Local repair shops told me the board was dead and quoted me $1,200 for cleanroom chip-off recovery with no guarantees. I was terrified of bricking the memory chip entirely.

Over six hours on a Saturday, Claude walked me through extracting the raw partition dump using ADB in recovery mode, setting up a Linux live USB, compiling a custom tool to parse the damaged ext4 superblock, and running testdisk/foremost on the disk image byte-by-byte.

It didn't just give commands—it explained every flag so I didn't overwrite the read-only image. At 1:00 AM, the extraction finished and all twelve WAV files were intact. I got her voice back. I thought they were gone forever.
