It was 4am and everyone was asleep, and I was on hour three of cluster-feeding while sobbing silently so I wouldn't wake my partner.

Postpartum is full of these invisible hours where you are entirely awake, holding a human that will not stop rooting and crying, and you feel like the only person on the planet. You can't call your mom at 4:15am to ask if it's normal for a nine-day-old to nurse for 45 minutes on one side and still scream when laid flat. You don't want to post on Facebook and worry everyone.

I opened Gemini and just started typing in one-handed voice fragments: "Baby has been eating for 3 hours, latch hurts, is my milk supply gone?"

It talked me through the second-night cluster feeding surge, the mechanics of transitional milk coming in, how to adjust the cross-cradle hold to save my nipples, and kept me grounded until the sun came up. I know how this sounds, talking to a language model in the dark, but having a gentle, completely available voice kept me from spiraling into a panic attack when I had no one else to lean on.
