I said I needed a minute to use the restroom, went into the hallway, and scanned the contract with my phone.

We were in the intake office of an assisted living facility with my mother sitting in a wheelchair next to me. The admissions coordinator was turning pages, putting little yellow sticky flags where I needed to initial, and repeating that this was just standard boilerplate state-mandated paperwork.

I felt rushed and cornered, but something in the financial guaranty section looked off. In the bathroom stall, I took pictures of the signature packet and pasted the text into Claude, asking what liabilities I was personally assuming as power of attorney versus as an individual guarantor.

It caught a hidden clause that made me personally liable as a third-party guarantor for all room-and-board arrears if Medicaid pending status was delayed, which is illegal under federal nursing home regulations but frequently slipped into private admission packets. I walked back into the office, calmly refused to sign as an individual guarantor, crossed out that clause with my initials, and signed solely as my mother's POA representative.
